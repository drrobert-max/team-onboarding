## What to keep

- Proactively and clearly explaining the direct-pay model and reimbursement-documentation option at the very top of the call, before any resistance appeared.
- Patient scheduling work around a real family conflict (a daughter's college move-in) without losing the booking.

## Leak points to fix

1. **This call and Mario & Mariano both show the price cave happening at the very front of the call, before discovery even starts** — a different failure pattern than a mid-call cave under sustained pressure. The fix here isn't "hold the line under pressure" so much as "don't answer a pricing question with a number at all, ever, regardless of when it's asked." Drill the validate → personalize → RoF → bridge sequence as the *only* response to any cost question, first ask or fifth.
2. **State the exam price correctly and consistently.** The $76/$67 mix-up is a small thing on its own, but paired with the much larger price-range slip, it reads as a call where numbers were not being handled carefully.
3. **Try harder to collect the deposit before deferring to a callback**, especially on a call where the family has already expressed price sensitivity — an unsecured booking after a sticker-shock moment is a high-risk no-show.
